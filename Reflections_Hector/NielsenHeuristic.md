# Nielsen Heuristics

Even though these design principles were developed almost 30 years ago and the technological scene has completely changed, I think they are still relevant, and some of them even more than before. 

---

### 1. Visibility of System Status
This principle is about keeping users informed about what is happening within the system through appropriate feedback at all times. It is not simply about loading speed, but about whether:
* An action was registered.
* A file was saved.
* A form was submitted.
* A process is currently running.

Nowadays, as interfaces handle increasingly complex and asynchronous operations, the absence of clear system feedback generates uncertainty. 

> **Key Research:** Studies in human–computer interaction show that users who lack status indicators are more likely to repeat actions, abandon tasks, or assume errors have occurred (**Nielsen, 1994; Shneiderman & Plaisant, 2010**).

---

### 2. User Control and Freedom
On the other hand, one principle I consider has suffered changes in recent years is **user control and freedom**. 

With modern AI-based interfaces making autonomous decisions (e.g., autocorrectors, code editors, and agents), users can lose control when actions are performed automatically, especially if there are no clear **undo** or **confirmation** mechanisms. 

* **Automation Bias:** Excessive or opaque automation can reduce user awareness and lead to frustration when something is hidden, modified, or deleted without the user's intention (**Parasuraman & Manzey, 2010; Amershi et al., 2019**).
