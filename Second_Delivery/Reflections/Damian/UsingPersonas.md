## Using Personas

The Personas Method is more valuable as a living hypothesis. According to Nielsen, 
personas based on assumption reflect the designer's intuitions about the users before 
any fieldwork is done. I asked for the AI to give 3 new proto-personas (Carlos, María 
and José) to do the analysis for this reflection, these three personas are manual or 
administrative workers, they were based on the assumptions that we could have of this 
profile of people before the interviews, obviously I checked, and I confirm that these 
was the assumptions that we had. So, the point of this reflection is that the Personas 
should not be used alone, but be used in tandem with other methods.

I compared the Personas information with the interview answers, the results are 
interesting; Some assumptions were confirmed, like the need for explicit feedback, 
every participant independently mentioned needing a notification to feel confident 
that an action was completed. Other assumptions required correction, these was the 
most, for example, the proto-personas assumed that users like José would abandoning 
the system silently after a failed interaction; the interview revealed that all three 
participants delegate to a trusted person rather than abandoning entirely. There were 
two assumptions that was missing entirely, and honestly, my team and I weren't 
expecting it, the first is that we assumed that they could had many problems 
remembering password, we were wrong and we sub estimated them, according with the 
interviews, all of them have no problem saving their password in the computer or 
writing in a notebook; other assumption that we failed, is that we overestimated the 
distrust that they could have in the applications, they said that they just need to 
have a user and a password to feel confident.  In summary, from eleven evaluated 
attributes, only three were confirmed, six require corrections, and two failed 
completely.

In conclusion, as we have seen, there is a difference between what is assumed and 
the reality; even though it may seem that we are largely correct, the reality is that 
everything we get wrong (which is a significative percentage) can lead to poor 
decisions in the UI; certain attributes might not be given the importance they need, 
or in the other side, we might overwork in attributes that perhaps don't require as 
much attention as we thought; all of this support the idea that while personas are a 
good tool for getting the big picture of what users are looking for, they do not work 
on their own and must be accompanied by user research, in this case, the interview.
