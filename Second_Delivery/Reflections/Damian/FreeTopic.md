## Feedback Loops

Users don't trust in what they can't see, one thing that all the workers that we
interviewed are agree is the need of explicit confirmation that an action was
completed. Every participant, independently, mentioned needing a notification
receipt, or on-screen message to feel confident that something worked. This is not
coincidence, is part of a fundamental principle in HCI theory.

Users need immediately feedback to know if their actions were successful, and this
need is specially critic in webs of low frequents use where they can't rely on past
experience. IxDF defines feedback loops as cycles where the user's actions generate
system responses that guide their behavior and decisions. Without those responses,
the system creates a gap that the user fills with uncertainty or abandonment. This
dynamic is clearly evident in our interviews. Verónica Ortega needs "a receipt
confirming that it's been done," and Julio César expects a confirmation email based
on his experience with the medical appointment system. Neither is expressing a
preference but rather the minimum requirement for using the system again. In the
CGRH, this translates to visible confirmations for each completed action: downloading
the CFDI, progress in the RF01 registration, and explicit logout, the latter being
especially critical for Julio, who uses a shared computer.

In conclusion, the feedback loops are not functionalities to be added once the system
is done, they are the mechanism through which trust is built or destroyed in each
interaction. For the CGRH system, where the target profile isn't familiarized with
digital self-service platforms and don't have a self-recovery strategy when something
goes wrong, the presence or absence of system clear feedback determines whether users
come back to the system or revert permanently to requesting their documents by email
or in person at the CGRH offices.
