# UI Design Patterns: Step-by-Step (Wizard) Pattern

UI Design Patterns are reusable solutions that address recurring challenges in interface design, and the Step-by-Step pattern, also known as the Wizard pattern, is one of the most widely recognized among them. This pattern breaks a complex process into a series of smaller, sequential steps, guiding the user through each stage one at a time. It is considered a best practice because it reduces cognitive load, helps users focus on one action at a time, and minimizes the risk of errors during multi-field interactions.

In our team project, the login screen is structured in two distinct stages that naturally align with this pattern, though it has not been formally implemented as such. In the first step, the user enters their employee ID and institutional email address. Before advancing to the second step, a validation process takes place to verify that the credentials exist and are recognized by the system. Only upon successful validation does the user proceed to the second step, where they set or confirm their desired password. Despite this two-part logic already being present in the flow, the interface does not currently reflect it with explicit progress indicators, step labels, or structured navigation controls that would make the sequence visible and clear to the user.

Formally applying the Step-by-Step pattern to this flow would significantly improve the overall usability of the login experience. A visible progress indicator, such as a two-step counter or a simple "Step 1 of 2" label, would give users a clear sense of where they are in the process and what to expect next. This directly supports learnability, as first-time users would immediately understand the structure of the login. Furthermore, making the validation step explicit reinforces error prevention, since users receive clear feedback before moving forward rather than encountering failures at the end of a completed form. Overall, formalizing this pattern would make the authentication flow more transparent, intuitive, and aligned with established usability standards.
