# User Research – Activities and Justification

## General Human Resources Coordination System (CGRH – UADY)

---

### Project Context

The CGRH system is designed to be used by all employees of the Universidad Autónoma de Yucatán (UADY). For the purposes of this academic project, the User Research process was originally planned to focus on retired employees over 60 years of age, given their specific characteristics such as basic or low digital literacy, possible visual decline, and difficulty remembering passwords.

However, during the execution phase, access to retired employees could not be secured within the available timeframe. The research was therefore redirected to **active employees** — both teaching staff and administrative personnel — who interact regularly with the CGRH system. This population still presents relevant characteristics for the study, including occasional use of digital platforms, heterogeneous levels of digital literacy, and shared reliance on institutional email as their primary channel for payroll and administrative information.

A total of **6 interviews** were conducted on March 20, 2026, with participants from two user segments: administrative staff (Personal Administrativo) and staff who work with institutional manuals (Manuales UADY).

---

## Activity Selected: Interviews

### Justification for Choosing Interviews as the Primary Method

Of the four originally planned activities — interviews, contextual inquiry, surveys, and usability testing — **interviews were selected as the sole method** for this research phase. This decision was based on the following reasons:

**Time and access constraints.** The research window was limited to a single session date. Coordinating contextual observation, distributing surveys, and running usability tests would have required extended access to participants and system infrastructure that was not available at this stage.

**Depth over breadth.** Given that the goal of this phase was to understand user needs, mental models, and pain points in order to justify design decisions, qualitative depth was prioritized over statistical coverage. Interviews provided richer, more actionable information than a survey could in the same amount of time.

**No functional prototype available.** Usability testing requires a working prototype or system to evaluate. Since the CGRH system is still in its design phase, usability testing will be conducted in a later iteration once a testable version is available.

**Flexibility of the method.** Interviews allowed the team to adapt questions in real time, probe for clarification, and follow unexpected threads that revealed important insights — something that surveys and structured observation would not have allowed.

---

### Interview Design

The interview guide was structured in three thematic blocks, with an estimated total duration of 36 minutes per session:

| Block             | Topic                   | Duration |
| ----------------- | ----------------------- | -------- |
| Block 1           | Technology use          | 5 min    |
| Block 2           | Current CGRH procedures | 10 min   |
| Block 3           | System expectations     | 16 min   |
| Opening / Closing | Consent and wrap-up     | 5 min    |

Each session was conducted by a team of three interviewers and one note-taker. Main questions were supported by follow-up probes to ensure depth of response when initial answers were brief.

---

### Expected Outcomes

- Main difficulties when using digital systems
- Prior experience with institutional platforms and email
- Needs related to navigation, error recovery, and password management
- General expectations about a centralized CGRH platform
- Sufficient data to identify usability attributes and design priorities

---

### Actual Outcomes

The interviews produced qualitative data across 8 thematic groups, analyzed through an affinity diagram. From this analysis, **4 critical usability attributes** were identified and selected as design priorities:

1. **Ease of navigation** — the most frequently mentioned pain point; complex or inconsistent navigation leads directly to task abandonment.
2. **Error handling and prevention** — the majority of users escalate or abandon tasks at the first sign of an error; the system cannot rely on users troubleshooting independently.
3. **System status visibility** — all 6 participants expect an explicit confirmation (on-screen and by email) after completing any action.
4. **Memorability** — given the infrequent nature of system use (biweekly for payroll, every 6 months for the Savings Fund), the interface must be re-learnable with minimal effort after periods of inactivity.

## Summary

This updated document reflects the decision to focus exclusively on semi-structured interviews as the research method for this phase. The change was driven by practical constraints — access, time, and the absence of a testable prototype — rather than a reconsideration of the value of the other methods. The interviews conducted provided sufficient qualitative evidence to define usability priorities and support design decisions for the CGRH system.
