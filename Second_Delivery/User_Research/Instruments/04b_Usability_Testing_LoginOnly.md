## 1. Evaluation Objectives

This version focuses exclusively on the login and password recovery flow of the CGRH system. It is the shortest version, designed for participants with very limited time or for sessions where the data visualization module is not yet prototyped.

- Identify comprehension problems in the login screen (labels, fields, buttons).
- Detect friction points in the password recovery flow.
- Validate assumptions about credential management in proto-personas (Armando, Feliciano).

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Modality** | In-person, waiting room, paper prototype |

| **Roles** | 1 facilitator (note-taker optional) |
| **Required materials** | Printed login + password recovery screens, task cards, log sheet, pencil |
| **Success criterion per task** | Participant correctly identifies the next action or target element on the screen |

---

## 2. How Paper Prototype Testing Works

The facilitator places printed screens in front of the participant one at a time. The participant points to what they would tap or click. The facilitator then places the next screen that would result from that action. The participant thinks aloud throughout.

**Important:** The facilitator does not tell the participant if they are right or wrong. They only swap screens based on what the participant points to.

## 4. Facilitator Script

### Opening (2 min)

**Suggested script** *(in Spanish)*:

> *"Gracias por su tiempo. Le voy a mostrar unas hojas impresas de una página web que estamos diseñando. Le pediré que me diga en voz alta qué haría en cada pantalla — qué tocaría o dónde haría clic. No estoy evaluando si usted sabe usar computadoras; estoy evaluando si la página está bien diseñada. No hay respuestas incorrectas. ¿Tiene alguna pregunta?"*

### During the session

- Do not confirm or deny if the participant's action is correct.
- If the participant asks "¿Está bien lo que hago?", respond: *"Haga lo que le parezca natural."*
- If the participant is stuck for more than 90 seconds, note the abandonment and move to the next task.
- Encourage think-aloud with: *"¿Qué está pensando? ¿Qué esperaría que pasara?"*

### Closing (2–3 min)

1. *(Spanish)* ¿Hubo algo en estas pantallas que le resultara confuso?
2. *(Spanish)* ¿Le quedaría claro cómo entrar al sistema si lo viera por primera vez?
3. *(Spanish)* ¿Qué cambiaría de la pantalla de entrada?

## 6. Usability Metrics (Adapted for Paper Prototype)

| Metric | Definition | Recording method |
|---|---|---|
| **Completion rate** | % of participants who correctly identify the target action | Log sheet |
| **Number of errors** | Incorrect points or elements selected before reaching the correct one | Observer records in real time |
| **Help requests** | Number of times participant asks the facilitator what to do | Count per task |
| **Qualitative issues** | Verbalizations of confusion, misread labels, unexpected expectations | Observer's text notes |

---

## 7. Finding Prioritization Criteria

| Level | Severity | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **4 – Critical** | Catastrophic | Participant cannot identify any correct action; task abandoned |
| **3 – High** | Major | Participant completes with significant errors or needs redirection |
| **2 – Medium** | Minor | Causes notable confusion or delay but participant reaches the goal |
| **1 – Low** | Cosmetic | Minor misread or hesitation; does not prevent task completion |
| **0 – No impact** | Not a problem | Observed but with no effect on the user experience |
