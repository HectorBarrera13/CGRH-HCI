# Traceability of Usability Attributes in the Prototype

For this reflection I am going with Intuitive Navigation, the attribute we identified as the highest priority in our user research and the one most directly visible in the decisions we made while building the system.

The current working build is organized around a landing page with four tabs, one for each benefit: CFDI, Fondo de Ahorro, Caja de Ahorro, and Fondo de Vivienda. Each tab contains a table with the relevant records and their info and download buttons. The reasoning behind this structure was to keep everything reachable in as few steps as possible, which maps directly to what we found in the interviews: users get lost when there are too many layers between them and the document they need. This structure was designed before the tests using our own knowledge along with assumptions and UI design principles, so we don't have any metrics to evaluate it yet. The prototype that will come out of the course will take this structure as a base and refine it using what we learn from the usability tests.

For measurement, the team defined metrics across two dimensions. The ones most relevant to navigation are task success rate, time on task, navigation error rate, and perceived orientation through a Likert item at the end of the session. These were chosen because they connect directly to the behaviors we observed in the interviews: users abandoning tasks, pressing back repeatedly, or losing track of where they were or want to go. The data would be collected through direct observation, screen recordings, and a short post session questionnaire. These metrics are very well known and have already stablished benchmarks, so we would be able to compare our results against those benchmarks to see how well the system is performing in terms of navigation.

As an alternative, the same attribute could be measured with a first-click test, where participants are shown the landing screen and asked where they would click to reach a specific goal. It is simpler to run, can be done remotely, and could fit the timeline better than a full moderated session if the time was more limited.
