# Reflection 1: Diseña tu ciudad reflection

On voting day for the Diseña tu Ciudad participatory budgeting platform, I observed and assisted family members of varying digital literacy levels as they attempted to register and vote through the web-based system. The most consistent point of failure was the registration process itself: the entry point from the Ayuntamiento website was not visually recognizable as interactive, and once inside, the CURP field caused significant confusion — most users could not locate the code on their INE card without help, and the form offered no contextual guidance. The OCR feature, intended to simplify data entry, created additional frustration before it was fixed due to unreliable reads and unclear error messages. Every person I observed required some form of verbal assistance to complete registration; none finished independently.

What this experience made clear from an HCI perspective is that the cumulative weight of small interface failures can effectively exclude motivated users from participating. These were people who wanted to engage with a civic process and still could not do so on their own. The system's learnability — its ability to communicate its own logic to a first-time user — was insufficient for a significant portion of its intended audience. For a democratic participation platform, that gap is not a minor inconvenience; it is a barrier to inclusion that targeted design improvements, such as a visual CURP reference, clearer affordances on the entry point, and a more robust or replaceable OCR flow, could meaningfully address.
