# Social Relevance

## CGRH Self-Service System – UADY

---

The General Coordination of Human Resources (CGRH) of the UADY seeks to provide its four main employee groups — retirees, faculty, manual workers, and administrative staff — with autonomous access to CFDI payroll receipts, the Retirement Savings Fund (FAR), and the Savings Fund (RF10, RF20, and RF30). Currently, this information is distributed primarily via email or upon individual request, a process that is unreliable and difficult to trace. In practice, employees frequently end up visiting the CGRH in person to obtain documents or resolve issues, creating unnecessary operational load for both staff and the coordination unit. A well-designed self-service system would consolidate access to this information in a single authenticated session, eliminating the dependency on email delivery and in-person visits across all four employee groups.

The HCI analysis will focus on retired employees over 60 years of age, the segment with the highest density of technological access barriers. This delimitation is methodologically advantageous: design criteria derived from the most demanding user profile benefit the system as a whole, on the premise that if an older adult can use it, the vast majority of other users will be able to as well.

The relevance of this segmentation is supported by statistical evidence. According to INEGI's ENDUTIH 2023, only 43.7% of the population between 55 and 64 years old used the internet in the past year, compared to 82.8% of the 25–34 age group. This structural gap justifies designing flows RF02 (password recovery) and RF03 (login) with cognitive accessibility criteria rather than conventions suited for users with high digital competence.

Additionally, retirees lack daily presence at university facilities, which amplifies their dependence on an autonomous channel to access financial information critical to their post-active life planning. Currently, an in-person procedure at the CGRH may involve travel, waiting time, and reliance on third parties for its management — friction that a well-designed system directly eliminates.
