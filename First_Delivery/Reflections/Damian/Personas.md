# Personas

The method of Personas is focused on give more comprehension about the user, going beyond the typical demographic data and the functional features that are usually used to design products. I chose to work with the category needs. I chose this category because in the CGRH system, the functional requirements are already defined, the system can display CFDIs, the Retirement Savings Fund, and the Savings Account. The problem is not what the system does, but if the user can actually use it.

The specific items I selected from the toolkit were practical needs, knowledge needs, and emotional needs, because they cover different capes of the same problem. The practical need of Don Armando, the 67-year-old retired professor who needs to download his CFDIs to present them as proof of income at a financial institution; then he just went to the place and in 10 minutes he had his receipt; now he need to do a process that he can´t do by him own. The knowledge need shows when Armando doesn´t understand what an error message means, and Feliciano doesn´t know what is an "user" or a "password". I believe the emotional need is really important and underrated, we can see it in the Concepción´s declaration: "I ask my daughter to help me because I can't do it on my own. But I also don't want to bother her every time I need a document."; that is a person that need to feel that she can do by her own, and feels that she not going to "broke" something.

The method we used to collect the items was the Contextual Inquiry, with this method we observed the users in their own environment with their computers, that is exactly what is needed to capture needs that the user would never be able to verbally say to us.

The best example of how people influenced the product is in RF02, the password recovery flow. Functionally, the flow is correct, but the person reveals something that the functional requirement can´t see, for the person, needs to recover a password is a conformation about the fear of doesn´t know to use the technology. It requires design decisions that would never appear in a requirements document based solely on functional data: the microcopy for RF02 must change from a neutral-technical tone to a calm and normalizing one, and the link expiration time must be generous (at least 60 minutes) because Armando checks his email infrequently and may take a while to find the message. None of this comes from the functional requirements. It all comes from knowing the people. That, for me, is the real value of the method.
